# Licensing and ownership: research result

## Summary

- **Confirmed: don't copy the document-skills code.** Anthropic's docx, pdf, pptx and xlsx skills are still source-available. Their LICENSE.txt is byte-identical locally and on GitHub. It forbids keeping copies outside Anthropic's Services, copying beyond automatic temporary copies, derivative works and distribution. So none of their code (validators, soffice.py, recalc.py) may go into the Apache-2.0 repository or a private archive without separate permission from Anthropic. pack.py and unpack.py no longer exist in the current repository.
- **skill-creator can be reused under Apache-2.0, and ships no NOTICE file.** 14 of the 19 skills in anthropics/skills carry an Apache-2.0 LICENSE.txt, and doc-coauthoring carries no licence file. skill-creator's published LICENSE.txt now reads "Copyright 2026 Anthropic, PBC.", which the local copy lacks. Reuse must meet Apache-2.0 section 4: include the licence, mark changed files, and keep the existing notices.
- **Take the MCE schema from docx4j, not from Anthropic's docx skill.** docx4j is Apache-2.0; its default branch is `VERSION_17_2_1` and its licence files are `legals/LICENSE` and `legals/NOTICE`. Anthropic's `mc.xsd` says it is "a modified version" of the docx4j file, but it sits under Anthropic's restrictive licence.
- **Transitional schemas: Ecma's ECMA-376 Part 4 zip is the clearest source, for unchanged copies that carry Ecma's notice.** Of the 5th-edition PDFs, only Part 3 prints that notice; Parts 1, 2 and 4 print none. Ecma's policy also says its copyright disclaimer does not let others "extract portions of the Standard". Ecma's BSD software licence is not applied to ECMA-376. ISO allows its electronic inserts to be used only "in their original format without any modifications", and its old free-standards site is closed.
- **Microsoft's extension schemas may be redistributed.** The Open Specifications notice on [MS-DOCX], [MS-XLSX] and [MS-PPTX] lets an implementation "distribute ... with or without modification, any schemas" in those documents. It grants no patent licence.
- **Microsoft Learn is CC BY 4.0 (text) and MIT (code), but only per MicrosoftDocs repository.** The grant covers "content in this repository". Otherwise the Learn Terms of Use limit reuse to personal, non-commercial use. Quotes and close paraphrases need CC BY 4.0 section 3(a) attribution.
- **Output ownership.** The Consumer Terms (effective 8 October 2025, still current) assign Anthropic's rights "if any" to the user. The Commercial Terms (effective 17 June 2025) cover Team, Enterprise and API use; under them the "Customer", which can be the organisation, "owns its Outputs". So output from an employer's Team or Enterprise account belongs to the employer as between Anthropic and the customer.
- **Copyrightability (US Copyright Office, 29 January 2025).** Purely AI-generated material is not protected, and prompts alone are not enough. Human expression, creative selection or arrangement, and creative modifications are protected. So Apache-2.0 can license only the human-authored parts, and any release from an employer must be a signed writing (17 U.S.C. 201(b) and 204(a)).
- **Use Apache-2.0 for code, not CC0.** OSI does not recommend CC0 for software, and Fedora has not allowed new CC0 code since 2022-08-01; both object to CC0's patent clause 4(a). Apache-2.0 is OSI-listed and on Fedora's allowed list. Its section 4 conditions apply when copies are reproduced and distributed.
- **The owner's CC0-1.0 standard can stay in the Apache-2.0 repository with its CC0 header.** CC0 imposes no notice or attribution condition. Creative Commons suggests a notice in each file plus a COPYING file. creativecommons.org does not address Apache-2.0 repositories specifically.

### A4. Microsoft Learn licences, and attribution when a skill quotes or paraphrases

- **Answer:** Confirmed, with one correction: Microsoft's statement applies per repository, since MicrosoftDocs repositories (for example microsoft-365-docs, azure-docs, office-developer-client-docs and win32) carry LICENSE (CC BY 4.0) and LICENSE-CODE (MIT), and the README grants "content in this repository" under CC BY 4.0 and "any code in the repository" under MIT. Outside such explicit terms, the Learn Terms of Use limit content to personal, non-commercial use, and Open Specifications pages such as [MS-DOCX] carry their own IP notice (see A2). When a skill quotes CC BY material or shares an adaptation of it, such as a close paraphrase, CC BY 4.0 section 3(a) requires the attribution the licensor supplies (creator, copyright notice, licence notice, disclaimer notice, link), a note of changes, and a notice with a link to CC BY 4.0; any reasonable manner suffices, and CC recommends giving Title, Author, Source and Licence (TASL). A paraphrase that takes only facts, procedures or methods falls outside copyright (17 U.S.C. 102(b)), and CC BY says it need not be complied with where an exception or limitation applies; attribution is then not legally required, though it remains good practice. ### A6. Ownership of work an employee creates with an employer-provided AI account

- **Answer:** Under US law, the employer is the author and owner of "a work prepared by an employee within the scope of his or her employment" unless the parties "expressly agreed otherwise in a written instrument signed by them", and any transfer of copyright ownership needs a writing signed by the owner. The Copyright Office's Circular 30 lists agency-law questions that decide employee status and scope: the skill required; whether the hiring party provided "the space, materials, or tools"; the length and control of the relationship; pay, benefits and tax treatment; and whether the work was done in business hours, as part of the creator's usual tasks and in authorised work time. An employer-provided AI account is a tool the employer supplies, and IP- or invention-assignment clauses and company policies can add employer rights. The AI terms point the same way: on an employer's Team or Enterprise plan the Commercial Terms make the organisation (the "Customer") owner of the Outputs, and under the Consumer Terms an account on an employer's email domain may be linked to the employer's enterprise account, whose administrator can then see Materials. General information only: ask the employer for a signed written statement that (1) names the repository and the kinds of work covered, including anything produced with the employer's Claude account; (2) either disclaims ownership, assigns or licenses the employer's rights to you, or authorises you to publish under Apache-2.0 on its behalf (the three routes Apache's contributor agreement recognises are permission, waiver, or a corporate agreement); (3) confirms that publishing breaches no confidentiality, acceptable-use or AI-use policy and that no confidential information is included; and (4) says who is named as copyright holder and whether the employer accepts the Apache-2.0 section 3 patent grant for any contribution it owns, bearing in mind that Apache itself says the answer depends on "their employer's ownership interests, applicable state and national law, and specific aspects of their employment contract and business policies".

### A7. Maximum reuse by companies: CC0 versus Apache-2.0

- **Answer:** I found no primary source that surveys corporate open-source policies, so whether companies "commonly" accept CC0 for code is not established. The policies I could read disfavour it: OSI does not recommend CC0 for software (it was never approved); Fedora has classed CC0-1.0 as allowed for content only since 2022-08-01, with existing code files grandfathered; the Apache Software Foundation, by contrast, lets CC0 works into Apache products with attribution. The objection is CC0 clause 4(a), under which no patent rights are "waived, abandoned, surrendered, licensed or otherwise affected": OSI feared it could "weaken patent infringement defenses" and Fedora cites a consensus against licences that "preclude any form of patent licensing", whereas Apache-2.0 section 3 grants a patent licence from each contributor that ends for anyone who sues over the Work's patents. Apache-2.0 is OSI-listed under "Popular / Strong Community" and on Fedora's "Allowed Licenses" list, and the Apache Software Foundation does "not distinguish between personal, internal, or commercial use", so it is broadly usable in public and private repositories. For purely internal use without distribution, section 4's conditions are framed around reproduction and distribution: (a), (c) and (d) refer to recipients or to what "You distribute", but (b), which requires marking modified files, is not worded that way; the section 3 patent termination and the section 6 trademark limit still apply, and "You" can include a company's affiliates, because "Legal Entity" includes entities under common control.

### A8. Including the CC0-1.0 PROJECT-BOOTSTRAP-AND-AUDIT standard in the Apache-2.0 repository

- **Answer:** Yes: the CC0 legal code waives all copyright and related rights "for any purpose whatsoever", and CC's FAQ says anyone can use a CC0 work "in any way and for any purpose" and that there is "no legal requirement that you attribute" the affirmer. So the file can sit in the Apache-2.0 repository with its CC0 header kept, and no notice is legally required. For software, Creative Commons and the Free Software Foundation suggest a short dedication notice at the top of each file plus a COPYING file holding the CC0 legal code, and CC calls CC0 "acceptable for software". creativecommons.org does not address Apache-2.0 repositories specifically, and it notes that CC0 "is not a license", so dual-licensing language does not apply to it. Because the repository's LICENSE says Apache-2.0, good practice (not required by any source) is to list the CC0 file as an exception in the README or licensing notes; Apache's FAQ also recommends tagging each file "in case they become detached from the LICENSE file", and the Apache Software Foundation's own policy, which does not bind this repository, asks for attribution when CC0 works go into its products.
